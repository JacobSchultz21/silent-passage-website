Mission

Distance is the easy part. Belonging is what takes years.

Every year, people make the decision to leave North Korea — a decision made in silence, often at enormous personal risk, and rarely with any guarantee of what comes after.

Silent Passage Project exists to make the "after" less uncertain. We focus on the parts of this journey that get the least attention: understanding what the process actually involves, knowing which organizations and channels are trustworthy, and building a support network that lasts well beyond arrival.

We are not a government agency and we do not conduct crossings. We work alongside the people, organizations, and communities already doing that work — pointing toward reliable information and helping others understand what's really at stake.

Roadmap

What we're building toward

We're a new, volunteer-run organization. These are the programs we're working to build partnerships and funding for — not services we operate today. We'll update this section honestly as each one moves from goal to reality.

Goal · Not yet active

Safe passage coordination

Working toward partnerships with established, trusted organizations already coordinating safe passage through third-party countries — so we can responsibly refer people rather than duplicate work that experienced groups already do well.

Goal · Not yet active

Initial resettlement support

Many people arrive without funds for housing, legal fees, or medical screening. We aim to eventually help fund these gaps directly — for now, we're building the financial and legal groundwork required to do that responsibly.

Goal · Not yet active

Interpretation, translation & mentorship

We want to pair new arrivals with professionals in relevant fields for 1:1 mentorship, alongside language support — helping people become self-reliant faster, not just housed.

Goal · Not yet active

Psychological recovery support

Years under state propaganda and the trauma of escape can leave lasting effects. We want to help connect people with qualified trauma therapy and language training — delivered by licensed professionals, not by us directly.

Need help right now, or know someone who does? These programs aren't active yet. Organizations already doing this work today include Liberty in North Korea (LiNK), PSCORE, and Crossing Borders — please reach out to them directly.
Team

Who's behind this

JS

Jacob Schultz

Founder

I moved to Korea wanting to make the most of my time here — to leave the country a little better than I found it, not just pass through it. That intention led me, almost by accident, to a restaurant run by a North Korean woman who'd escaped the country decades earlier. Over the course of one meal, she told me what the crossing had actually cost her — and that even thirty years later, she was still adjusting. I paid my bill, walked out, and knew I'd found the cause I wanted to spend my time on. Silent Passage Project started from that conversation.
