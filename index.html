Silent Passage Project

Getting out of North Korea is only the first border.

We assist people escaping North Korea and stand beside them through what follows: financial independence, safety from exploitation, psychological recovery, and a lasting support network.

Mission

Distance is the easy part. Belonging is what takes years.

Every year, people make the decision to leave North Korea, a decision made in silence, often at enormous personal risk, and rarely with any guarantee of what comes after.

Silent Passage Project exists to make the "after" less uncertain. We provide direct support across the parts of this journey that get the least attention: coordinating safe passage, funding the costs of resettlement, and building a support network that lasts well beyond arrival.

We are not a government agency. We work directly with trusted networks in border regions and in resettlement communities to deliver this support, and we're upfront on this page about what's operational today versus what we're still building.

What We're Doing

Direct support, at every stage

We're a volunteer-run organization providing direct support at each stage of this journey: from coordinating passage, to funding resettlement, to staying involved well after arrival. Here's what's operational today.

Safe passage coordination

We work with trusted intermediaries in border regions to coordinate safe passage through third-party countries before final resettlement.

Initial resettlement funds

Upon arrival, escapees often lack funds for housing, legal fees, and medical screening. We help provide sponsorship to support these often-overlooked costs.

Interpretation, translation & mentorship

Interpretation and translation services, paired with 1:1 mentorship matching with professionals in relevant fields, help people get acclimated and become self-reliant in society.

Psychological recovery

Escapees suffer from PTSD and cognitive dissonance due to years of state propaganda. We provide access to therapy and language training, delivered by licensed professionals.

Our Pledge

To those who've made the crossing, and to those still deciding to

We see you as we would want to be seen ourselves: not as a statistic, not as a cautionary tale, but as someone building a life worth living.

We believe the right to think freely, speak freely, and go where you choose isn't something to be earned. It is already yours.

We believe no one should have to risk their life to claim rights that should have been theirs from the start.

We cannot know what the crossing has cost you. What we can do is keep showing up, in whatever ways are available to us, long after the story stops making headlines.

This is what we are committing to: funding what we can, connecting who we can, and staying involved for as long as it takes.

Who's behind this

JS

Jacob Schultz

Founder

I found this cause over a bowl of soup. The woman who made it had escaped North Korea and between orders she told me what the crossing had cost her. Thirty years later, she was still adjusting. I kept thinking about it long after I left. Silent Passage Project exists so no one who makes that crossing has to carry what comes after alone.

MP

Michelle Paras

Director of Women's Safety

After a summer with PSCORE, an NGO working directly with North Korean defectors, I saw how little dedicated support exists for trafficking survivors. Human trafficking shapes the majority of female defectors’ escapes, yet the gendered violence they face is almost completely overlooked in resettlement policy and public discussion. I joined the Silent Passage Project to document North Korean women’s stories, focusing on what they cannot outrun by crossing the border. I hope to provide a fuller picture of what defection entails and how to best support their path to freedom.
